# November 13, 2026 — The original NASA SPOC PRF difference-image results do NOT independently recover the two reported ground-neighbor sources

**New mission-grade source comparator:** All **12 original authentic NASA SPOC Data Validation XML reports** for our two ground-aperture-sky-verified ExoFOP NEB cases have been downloaded and source-byte SHA256 recorded: **6 different, overlapping official mission search reductions for TOI-1223.01 and 6 for TOI-2250.01**. Each official XML contained **exactly one TCE with an original NASA TPS orbital period and transit phase matching the unchanged published ExoFOP TOI** under the threshold fixed **before reading any PRF source positions** (period disagreement ≤1%, cyclic phase difference ≤min(0.15 orbital periods, 3 published transit durations)). We then examined the official first-observing-sector NASA difference-image point-response-function (PRF) sky centroid, and only when the original NASA quality flag says the fit was attempted and valid and its reported RA/Dec uncertainties were nonnegative.

**Actual result:** **6 of the 12 repeated official PRF fits were marked valid (5 for TOI-1223.01, 1 for TOI-2250.01); all six valid fits had centroids NOMINALLY nearer the bright primary TIC than the reported ground-observed eclipsing neighbor TIC.** Six other source fits were *invalid*, not zero-offset observations. These are **two distinct stars**, not 12 independent source samples. This does NOT establish that either eclipse occurred on the primary. In fact, original on-time ground-telescope aperture photometry and separately plate-solved real-image source matching support a neighboring star's dimming, while the TESS difference-image fits are weak/unstable in a badly blended scene.

### Source-level interpretation

**TOI-1223.01:** This has independently source-associated ground-aperture eclipsing TIC382437045 about 24.5″ from the primary. The earliest archived original NASA search (sector 1–13) returned a valid PRF centroid **3.35″ from the primary**, compared with **21.37″ from the ground-reported neighbor**. The four later valid original reductions shifted the first-sector fitted location to **~32–35″ from primary** and **~46–47″ from neighbor**, even though the earlier source identity and first source sector were unchanged. One other archived source reduction was formally invalid. This shows significant **dependence on overlapping mission search/reprocessing**, not an invariant 3.35″ exact astrophysical source localization. Our own first-sector raw TPF true-event difference image had only **two** complete event windows, and a fixed `−P/4` pseudo-event had a larger pixel response than the actual event, cautioning against source claims.

**TOI-2250.01:** Its ground T11 aperture was matched to independently cataloged TIC1884344804 about 31.5″ from the host, with a **259.75 ppt** original ground brightness dip. A simple full-flux two-star dilution model predicted a **92.5 ppm** TESS dip, quite close to the actual **98.7 ppm** calibrated TESS observation. But the **earliest original NASA DV search (sector 14–23) marked its first-sector difference-image PRF fit invalid**, and **four of five** later source reruns also marked it invalid; in the only later valid run (14–26), the fitted PRF location was **28.06″ from primary** versus **58.99″ from ground neighbor**. Our independently coded original TPF pixel-difference-image positive center was also nearer the primary, but its total signed raw pixel difference was slightly negative. Thus the two independent *TESS* source-location estimators do **not nominally recover** the independently ground-reported faint neighbor.

### Science conclusion, explicitly no accuracy claims

- **Original NASA SPOC officially detects both matching time-period signals,** proven by exact original TCE period and phase matching in all 12 archived XML runs. This does not by itself establish planetary origin.
- **Neither a simplistic original uncalibrated TPF difference image nor the available NASA official valid first-sector PRF sky positions nominally place the inferred source closer to the externally identified nearby star.** This can arise because faint nearby eclipsing stars sit ~1–1.5 native TESS pixels from bright primaries, because of systematics, because of source-model complexity or pipeline reduction changes, or because the true TESS signal has a more complex astrophysical explanation than the available source notes alone resolve.
- **This is NOT proof that the neighboring ground-aperture eclipses did not occur or that TESS detected an on-target planet.** Original ground plate-solved imaging and real time-series photometry remain valid independent evidence of off-target dimming, but cannot by themselves establish that no other signal contributed to TESS's time series.
- **No generalizable detection/classification improvement** is demonstrated for the experimental `MOM_CENTR-POS_CORR` point-subtraction. The original NASA ExoMiner++ classifier weights, model scores, planet labels and official sky-source fit outputs were untouched.
- **Two original host sources, not twelve:** no p-value or claims of classification sensitivity/specificity from these retrospective two astronomical cases. The earliest source campaign and all invalid results stay in the analysis.
