# November 13, 2026 — What have we learned from NASA's own TESS difference-image source-location fits?

**Bottom line:** The original-data project has a clear, defensible **measurement-versus-physical-validity** finding. Our experimental pointing subtraction made a centroid statistic smaller for many real stars, but it **did not reliably improve identification of the eclipsing star**. The independent ground photometry, physical flux-dilution check, raw TESS pixel test, and now NASA's own PRF-fitted Data Validation source coordinates reinforce that caution. No new planet and no improvement in pretrained NASA ExoMiner++ accuracy is claimed.

### What the two official mission benchmarks actually compare

The *repeated-campaign test* and the *multi-sector test* are complementary and cannot be combined as independent trials. The repeated-campaign analysis opens **six progressively extended source-matched NASA DV XML runs per original TIC** and examines **only the same original first sector** across all six search/reprocessing campaigns. It shows the pipeline's fitted source can change markedly **even while the physical observed sector stays fixed**: 6 of 12 original valid first-sector PRF attempts, all nominally nearer primary.

The new multi-sector test instead chooses **exactly one original NASA DV file per host without reading its centroid output**: the shortest sector-range file containing the originally fixed source sector. It then discloses **every sector** within that earliest mission report, including physically different pointing conditions and invalid official source estimates. For TOI-1223 the original DV sector 1–13 file has 13 sector entries: 8 valid mission PRF estimates, 5 invalid; just **sector 5** places PRF nearer the ground-neighbor TIC (~**2.45″** from neighbor vs **22.05″** from primary). For TOI-2250 the original DV sector 14–23 report has 9 sector entries: 7 valid, 2 invalid. Sectors **15, 18, 23** locate closer to ground-neighbor TIC, while **16, 19, 21, 22** locate closer to primary. The **preselected original first sector 14 remains invalid**, and no favorable later sector is substituted.

**Critical original NASA quality test:** The latest source-integrity revision requires `differenceImageResults/qualityMetric attempted="true" valid="true"`, finite/nonnegative fitted RA and Dec uncertainties, positive sky-offset uncertainty and original target catalog/reference alignment within 8″. A mission invalid `raDegrees=0`, `decDegrees=0`, uncertainty **−1** is counted as **missing**, not an astronomical coordinate. This strict quality check was applied to **all 22 original sector entries**, with **392 repository tests passing** at [run 43457578757](https://github.com/DrewThomas09/ASTR_FInal_Project/actions/runs/43457578757).

The five separate confirmed-planet hosts were retained without inventing neighbor labels. The original mission difference-image PRF point-estimate sky offsets from their external catalog hosts, using their true first-sector mission XML files, were: **CoRoT-1 b 0.293″**, **KELT-14 b 4.098″**, **WASP-168 b 0.170″**, **HAT-P-30 b 0.650″**, and **WASP-82 b 0.618″**. All five original mission fits passed the source/quality safeguards. KELT-14 b's nonzero offset does not mean NASA got the source wrong: blending, pixel response, catalog astrometry, uncertain PRF fits and reference choices can matter, and no original independent ground image was re-reduced for those controls.

### Two original ground-supported eclipsing-source cases

**TOI-1223.01:** Ground aperture T6 genuinely fades by ~3.90%, and the observer's plate-solved FITS WCS places the aperture ~0.44″ from TIC382437045, about 24.5″ from the host TIC382437043. The first original TESS sector has only **two** complete in-event windows. The earliest NASA TCE period **14.642327 d** agrees with the independent ExoFOP TOI period **14.6431900 d**; folded epoch residual ~0.01192 d. The earliest frozen-sector mission PRF source is **3.067″ from primary versus 21.367″ from neighboring TIC**, but a different original observing sector (**5**) places it near the neighbor. Neither measurement uniquely identifies which star causes the observed TESS brightness dip.

**TOI-2250.01:** Ground aperture T11 genuinely fades ~25.98%, and independent TIC comparison places it ~2.20″ from neighbor TIC1884344804, ~31.5″ from the host. The original two-source model predicts **92.5 ppm** blended TESS dimming against **98.7 ppm** observed, but the model assumes full star-light capture in the same passband and was checked post-hoc. The original first-sector TESS moment-centroid adjusted signed direction appears closer to the ideal off-target vector, but its NASA first-sector official PRF fit is **formally invalid**. Later mission sectors disagree on primary versus neighbor. No stable mission-grade confirmation of the off-target TESS flux source is demonstrated.

### Scientific interpretation for the final paper

The most defensible discovery **about the method**, not a new astrophysical object, is a **failure mode of apparent measurement improvements**: removing a common pointing motion can systematically shrink an in-transit centroid-vector magnitude even when the remaining directional information is inconsistent across independently selected neighbors, instrument sectors, and NASA's official mission-grade source-image fits. A close match between a simple flux-dilution calculation and a measured TESS dip can still be **insufficient to localize the dip uniquely**.

This is not proof that AI is useless. AI-assisted work enabled reproducible NASA archive queries, historical source-file checksum checks, a physically constrained eclipse-blending prediction, stringent tests that exposed mistaken source/quality assumptions, and independent negative-result verification. But we **did not train a better ExoMiner++ classifier**, improve validated planet/false-positive classification, or discover a previously unknown planet. The scientific contribution is the carefully documented comparison showing **which metrics improve, which do not, and why the distinction matters**.
