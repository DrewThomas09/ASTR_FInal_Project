# November 12, 2026 — Original NASA/TESS TPF eclipse difference-image cross-check is NOT a reliable off-target localization

**Critical new genuine original-data result:** After finding two ground-telescope neighbor apertures with real dimming, matching their sky TIC positions, and finding that both neighbor sky points are *within* the original TESS SPOC photometric/centroid masks, I tried a direct **pixel-level check**. For the exact previously frozen first-sector original NASA/TESS target-pixel FITS files, I made a simple source-preserving difference image: median of actual **pre/post-event pixel flux** minus median of actual **inside-event pixel flux**. I repeated the exact calculation in the two predeclared **±P/4 pseudo-event windows**, using the same original moment-centroid-selected detector pixels, without changing star, time, sector, mask, or detector calibration.

In the true-event average difference images, the brightest selected detector pixel **and** a positive-only flux-weighted center both lay **closer to the bright primary target star than to the independently ground-aperture-sky-matched neighboring TIC**. This conflicts with the tempting assumption that a naive positive-only pixel difference image will automatically point closest to the fading neighbor. **It does NOT establish a primary-star eclipse**: the original raw pixel out-minus-in signal is weak, phase controls have comparable false structure, and the TESS native resolution does not separate these sources well. It demonstrates an additional **failure of simple localization estimators**, despite the earlier appealing blended-flux numerical agreement.

**Especially important:** In TOI-2250, the selected-pixel *signed total* of the median difference image is **slightly negative** (−0.209 TPF flux units), even though a subset of positive pixels sums to **+2.433** units and the calibrated `PDCSAP_FLUX` source light-curve median dip is **+98.7 ppm**. That means the raw TPF *simple unsystematics-corrected difference image* does **not even provide a clean, net positive eclipse difference** under this estimator. A center calculated after clipping all negative residuals to zero is therefore liable to be dominated by non-astrophysical noise, background, and bright primary-star artifacts. Calling it a recovered star location would be scientifically incorrect.

TOI-1223 had only **two** genuine source event images, and retains its prespecified **ineligible** designation. Its **−P/4** pseudo-event fixed phase control had **+26.17 original TPF signed source flux units** and **+132.53 positive-clipped units**, substantially larger than the actual event image signed sum **+8.45** and clipped **+13.90**. Such a false-phase response points to major variability/systematic uncertainty and makes any claim that two raw pixel differences can uniquely locate the off-target source untenable.

For TOI-2250, although there were **30 genuine original event images** and 31 and 32 valid fixed-control pseudo-event images, the controls had positive-clipped source differences **+1.232** and **+1.345** units, versus **+2.433** in the true-event image, with their own peaks also closer to the primary. A larger positive-clipped true-event sum could reflect faint astrophysical eclipse signal, but is not equivalent to locating the source. The true-event selected signed source image total remains slightly negative.

These results are an **honest non-replication of simplistic image-based localization**, rather than evidence that an off-target event was actually on target. The independent ground observation of a deep eclipse at T11, its plate-solved sky match to TIC1884344804, the original neighbor's T-band magnitude and the close **92.5 ppm idealized versus 98.7 ppm measured** TESS flux agreement all remain independently sourced. The raw pixel-image heuristic is too crude and noisy to choose between primary and neighbor at **1.5 native pixels**.

## Why this changes the research conclusion

The student project now has three distinct measurement problems:
1. **Local instrument stability:** the experimental `MOM_CENTR-POS_CORR` subtraction reduced some local drift proxies but worsened others, including the confirmed-planet CoRoT-1 b host.
2. **Signed moment-centroid physical vector direction:** for one selected, ground-supported NEB (TOI-2250) the correction moved the observed source vector toward the physically expected away-neighbor direction, but TOI-1223 was ineligible with two events and would directionally worsen.
3. **Independent pixel-level eclipse localization:** directly constructing a non-PRF target-pixel out-minus-in image from the original observed data did **not** return a robust nearby-source centroid/peak; in TOI2250 the signed pixel difference sum failed the net-positive check, and in TOI1223 the false-phase control was larger than the true window.

Therefore **we cannot claim improved planet classification or successful generalized off-target source localization**, even with a very close post-hoc binary-dilution prediction and a favorable signed MOM centroid for one host. A real professional next step would require the official NASA SPOC Data Validation difference-image PRF source positions (when available), calibrated per-pixel uncertainty/background PRF modeling, or independent ground pixel-level astrophysical reduction. Do not tune the present simple estimator or choose alternate sectors post-hoc to force a better-looking conclusion.
